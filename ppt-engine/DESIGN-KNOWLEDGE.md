# Presentation design knowledge: real templates and working methods

Research date: 26 September 2026. Purpose: improve the ProjectArrakis deck engine's *design decisions*, not give it another fixed set of cards. This is a source audit and an implementation guide, not a claim that the engine already implements these methods.

## Evidence and method

I downloaded the four official SlidesCarnival PowerPoint files, parsed their slide/master/theme OOXML and native editable objects with `python-pptx`/`lxml`, then rendered selected slides through LibreOffice for a visual cross-check. Dimensions below are from native shapes in inches on a 20 x 11.25 inch 16:9 canvas, not measurements guessed from a thumbnail. Each source's template page and official PPTX download route are listed below. Slidesgo's Gray & Neon page advertises PowerPoint, but its `/download` endpoint sent the unauthenticated request to an identity-provider 403; its findings here are **preview/page only**, not OOXML. Slidesgo says free use requires retaining its credits slide. These decks contain demonstration claims, names and numbers; those are not factual sources for new content. [SC1-SC4, SG1]

Important mechanical distinction: these exported decks put **every content slide on a BLANK layout** despite carrying 9-11 layouts and a master. Much of what looks like a colored or photographic card is a *group of freeforms and image fills*, not a simple `PICTURE` or rectangular fill. A top-level picture count misses embedded/grouped image layers. The SlidesCarnival Google-exported files' `theme1.xml` sometimes retains default colors such as `058DC7` even where the visible slide uses explicit local RGB overrides. **Read actual slide/background/group XML and pixels; never derive a palette solely from theme colors or top-level picture objects.** This is also why the following counts describe explicit runs/top-level shapes, not the complete visual composition. ## Anatomy of each deck: geometry, hierarchy, pace and intended use

### 1. Sales Kickoff: the high-impact reference [SC1]

- **System:** 20 x 11.25 in, white canvas with local `EDEDED` pale panels. Explicit local hues include lime `76E367`/yellow `EAEC47`, cyan `89FFFE`/`57C7FF`, peach `FF8C4C`/`FFE27B`, lavender `AF70FF`, and pink `FF8CED`. The colors are applied to field/panel groups and images, not held to one flat brand accent. Black type keeps these aggressive colors readable. Inter's explicit run sizes range from 18-48 pt small/body/display to 88-104 pt section heads; cover title reaches **170 pt**; huge monetary proof uses **179.5 pt**. These are literal dimensions on the 20-inch canvas, not a recommendation to use 170 pt on a 13.33-inch slide.
- **Grid:** cover has two near-full-height panels: left x=0.41 to 11.20, right x=11.58 to 19.61, giving 0.38 in gap and ~0.4 in outside edge. The title starts x=1.41. Agenda slide has four equal cards x=0.41, 5.35, 10.18, 15.00, width 4.61, nominal ~0.22-0.33 in gutters; each card has a small circular sequence number and bottom-anchored topic. Main section divider 4 has image panel x=0.41 to 8.44, text panel x=6.03 to 19.62: they **overlap deliberately** by 2.41 in, allowing the 3D asset to intrude into the text-side boundary. Treat this as intentional layered spatial composition, not an overlap bug.
- **6-up data rhythm:** slide 5 uses three columns x=0.41, 6.93, 13.47 with widths 6.13; rows top y=0.34, lower y=5.8-5.84, height ~5.08. The ~0.4 in horizontal gutters and ~0.4 in row gap remain constant; alternating metric/text cards with colorful 3D assets makes a grid lively rather than a sterile metric dashboard. The number and its label occupy different scale levels. Slide 9 contrasts two wide metric rows x=0.41 to 13.29 with a narrow full-height image panel x=13.62 to 19.61.
- **Rhetorical sequence:** cover (1), four-part agenda (2), full quotation (3), section transition (4), six-cell proof (5), giant hero metric + 3D (6), vision transition (7), office/aspiration (8), paired targets (9), next-steps transition (10), four actionable columns (11), closing quote (12), thanks/contact (13-14), credits/resource slides (15-16). This isn't "card slide x 16": hero, divider, grid, object-led, and compact action layouts alternate. The 3D objects supply movement and tactility without needing every slide to be busy.
- **Use in Arrakis:** preserve the *principle* of one recognizably strong focal asset, powerful asymmetry, two pace changes after dense sequences, and consistent gutters. Do not copy its 3D assets or sales labels into another topic. Avoid duplicating slide 5's under-tested numeric typesetting: explicit fit/preview checks are required. Text and many groups are native/editable; imported 3D artwork is raster media, not native vector geometry.

### 2. Corporate Sales Pitch: persuasion by restrained structure [SC2]

- **System:** 20 x 11.25 in. Local ink `0F1A38`, pale-blue field `E4F3FF`, vivid blue `3686FD`, mint `51EDAD` accents, white. Dominant Inter/Inter Light with 80 pt headlines, 56 pt agenda items, 28 pt body/labels, 18 pt navigation. Cover has left text x=0.73, hero face and graphic on approximately the right 9.85-20.68 in; picture boxes can extend *past* the edge as a crop. Content slide headers usually align x≈1.99; tiny "Back to agenda page" anchors x=0.73, y=10.2. This difference is a deliberate two-tier margin system, not random padding.
- **Structure:** slide 1 personality-led cover; 2 numbered narrative agenda; 3 problem has three blue outlined circles beneath a short horizontal set-up; 4 cost has three outlined containers, each about 4.8 x 3.35 in at x=1.99, 7.60, 13.21 (0.81 in gutters); 5 is a half-image introduction; 6 mission headline and photo; 7 audience fit; 8 process; 9 has three long testimonial containers at x=1.99, 7.62, 13.25, each 4.8 x 6.28; 10 a stage chart; 11 three-step CTA; 12 thank-you; 13-15 attribution/resources. Pale blue creates occasional breathing room between predominantly white analytic pages.
- **Design logic:** confidence comes from crisp alignment and repeated line weight, not a dozen unfilled cards. Photography appears where a human/product story is introduced, while outlined geometries organize problem, proof and quotes. The x≈2 in content grid is much roomier than Sales Kickoff's edge-packed grid. A good style choice for a sober pitch, not a stand-in for the playful Sales Kickoff look.
- **Quality warning:** the rendered split `Introducin/g` on slide 5 and dense small type elsewhere show that imported designs still need content-aware line breaks, font presence and viewer-specific testing. The slide 10 chart is a top-level picture, not an editable chart, so deck-level editability does not mean each data graphic is editable.

### 3. SaaS Sales Proposal: vivid modular system with anchoring chrome [SC3]

- **System:** 20 x 11.25 in, local full-slide backgrounds: charcoal `1C1C1C` (cover/agenda), brick `D44B43`, coral `ED7843`, yellow `FFDC4D`, blue `70B2E2`, pink `FF8EBA`, cream `FFFAF0`, green `53B080`, lilac `C788FF`. Bungee display + Inter/Inter Medium; common explicit run sizes 17, 20, 24 pt, with 75-109 pt section heads. The 0.73 in left margin, x=0.73 header/logo x≈0.63 y, and y=9.71 lower navigation/recipient pill anchor virtually every page even as field colors change.
- **Grid and composition:** cover photo at x=12.12, y=1.35, w=6.87, h=8.55; hero title x=0.73, y=3.46, width 9.94. Slide 2 uses nine compact agenda cells in a 3 x 3 block x≈10.68/13.61/16.55, y≈2.35/4.43/6.51, each ~2.72 x 2.13. Slide 5 places the two comparison columns x=4.09 and 10.18, width 5.73, with ~0.36 in gap. Slide 6 stacks four process bands x=11.04, width 7.83, height 1.70, y=2.02, 3.77, 5.53, 7.28. Slide 9 uses four timeline columns starting x=1.73, 5.90, 10.09, 14.27; widths ~4.01, gutters ~0.17. Thin central rules knit these contrasting grids into one system.
- **Purpose/rhythm:** agenda > context > client snapshot > challenges/solutions > steps > competitive context > testimonial > implementation > price > benefits > CTA, followed by credits. Display-led introduction and colorful short-sequence/process/price slides supply rhythm. Repeated pills and footer are intentionally stable while the central composition varies. Don't merely repaint a card deck in bright colors: its contrasts and sequence do the work.
- **QA lesson:** the small 17-20 pt descriptive text in the 20-inch design may become too small after scale-down/export. A complete proposal needs stable place for client, price and scope, but demonstration content cannot be reused as a factual claim.

### 4. Minimalist Grayscale: editorial restraint, not empty slides [SC4]

- **System:** 20 x 11.25 in, background `FAFAFA`, text usually `545454`, dark `000000`/`222222`, light rules and monochrome photos. Theme actually aligns better with the visible palette here (`dk1=545454`, `lt1=FAFAFA`, etc.). Ledger display and Arial body are prominent; many explicit headline runs 70 pt, subhead 45/32 pt, body 22 pt; section divider 115.2 pt. No saturated accent carries hierarchy. Repetition of a thin top rule from near x=0 to ~16 in, folio at x=16.11, y=0.67, photo placement and deliberate photo/white-space alternation create identity.
- **Grid:** cover layers a grayscale photo across x=0 to 10 in and an independent image x=12.90, y=1.93, w=4.11, h=7.56; white text zone floats across them. Agenda has two images x=10.19, width 8.10, y=2.02 and 6.44. Company slide 5 has a panoramic photo x=0.92, y=6.54, w=17.36, h=3.59. Problem/solution slide 6 puts tall image x=7.10, y=1.92, w=5.81, h=8.20 and places text on both sides. Gallery slide 9 is a five-photo asymmetrical collage, with narrow vertical strip and staggered smaller images rather than equal cards. Market slide 10 has text left and stacked photography on the right. Section dividers 3/7/14 intentionally spend most of the canvas on imagery + a single oversized title.
- **Slide purpose:** editorial title > agenda > section > welcome/company > problem/solution > project section > services/gallery > market/numbers/SWOT > team/about/history > pricing/timeline > full-image pause > quote > contact > attribution. A rich *layout library* exists even though no bright colors or abundant ornament do. Several slides contain lorem ipsum or instructional copy and must be rebuilt for real facts.
- **Risk:** Ledger may not be installed on the target system, and the 22 pt body on 20-inch canvas may shrink under a 13.33-inch rebuild. Keep the typographic *ratio* and editorial crop patterns, not raw point sizes without adaptation.

### 5. Slidesgo Gray & Neon: explicitly preview-only [SG1]

## What people actually do: two different workflows

### RAW from blank: story first, then visual construction

1. **Define the audience change and central argument.** Duarte's audience-journey article asks who the audience is before/after, writes the talk track first, then breaks it into single ideas; BrightCarbon says to find the exact point a visual slide must make before drawing it. This is a planning step, not a choice of title text color. [D3, B2]
2. **Externalize and reorder ideas.** Duarte describes placing one point per sticky note, grouping/rearranging notes and printed existing slides on a board, then sketching concepts before executing in software. Pencil sketches can be rough; the purpose is option generation, not polished illustration. [D1, D2]
3. **Choose the visual *verb* for each idea.** BrightCarbon's worked method identifies actors/objects, the setting (e.g. process, map, graph, timeline) and what changes (movement, growth, comparison, substitution). A human doesn't convert all ideas into `headline + 3 cards`; a claim about change calls for before/after, a journey calls for route/sequence, a comparison calls for aligned alternatives. [B2]
4. **Set a small visual system, then design hero and body examples.** Decide canvas, palette roles, type pairing, page grid, image treatment and motif. Make one strong lead slide and a representative analytical slide; judge them at presentation size before multiplying variants. For a *functional reusable template*, BrightCarbon recommends master/layouts, defaults, contrast checks and a simple 3 x 3 grid with margins. It warns against changing size after slides exist. [B1]
5. **Build by transformation, not blind duplication.** One video transcript demonstrates a common practical blank-to-deck tactic: create background, decorative corner motif and type; duplicate a working slide and replace image/chart/number for later beats. This is a *mechanical speed tactic*; the tutorial barely addresses research or original narrative, so pair it with the story-led sources above. Its repeated motif can support consistency, but ten near-identical pages would have poor rhythm. [V1, D3, B2]
6. **Render, critique, revise.** In an editable deck, text and diagrams need native objects, but that promise must be tested. Verify hierarchy and meaningful whitespace, inspect all rendered slides at size, change the composition when content does not fit. BrightCarbon explicitly advises contrast checks on different displays, including a projector, and recommends not hiding overflow behind silent auto-fit. [B1]

### TEMPLATE adaptation: audit and translate, not type into every slot

1. **Inventory the source and its audience.** BrightCarbon's template lead describes auditing old decks and interviewing users to learn which layout types people need. For an incoming template, inspect *all* pages, recurring logo/footer/navigation, masters/layouts, editable vs image objects, font dependencies, source media rights, and credits. Map the new story to only the layouts that serve it. [B3, B1]
2. **Extract grammar separately from content.** Decode field colors, grid/alignment, hero asset behavior, headline/body contrast, corner treatment and sequence rhythm. Record keep/translate/reject. A sales template's placeholder numbers, product labels and stock photos are not evidence for an unrelated story. A literal branded-template requirement is different from style inspiration: preserve required marks when the user expects compliance. [SC1-SC4]
3. **Edit at the right level.** In real template tutorials, repeated organization names and URLs sometimes live on the slide master, while individual copy/images live on slides. BrightCarbon's authoring guide distinguishes theme color/font, master, layout and normal-slide defaults, and notes that a blank layout is important when pasting slides. But our four source PPTX files have effectively *flattened master usage*: all content slides select BLANK and much visible decoration is per-slide grouped; editing the master alone won't replace their repeated badges. Inspect XML/object hierarchy before deciding whether to mutate a master, replace a slide-level group or construct a fresh design system. [E1, B1, SC1-SC4]
4. **Select, prune and recompose.** An Envato Tuts+ walkthrough chooses particular predesigned slides (intro, team, services, success story, contact), swaps images/copy, changes master-level repeated details and deletes unneeded icons. That is a realistic fast path for a topic that matches the available layouts. A higher-quality adaptation may move a text panel, change the image crop, reflow the title, or invent a compatible diagram when the source layout cannot hold the new claim. Do not pad the story merely to use all 16 or 22 slides. [E1]
5. **Protect the original's constraints while verifying real output.** Recheck slide sequence, licenses/attributions, type availability, resizing, grouped raster/embedded objects, chart editability and after-export display. The sampled LibreOffice failures above demonstrate why a native source deck isn't a guaranteed finished design. [SC1-SC4, B1]

## Engine implications: practical next design contracts

These are recommendations derived from the audited decks and designer sources, not claims of implemented features.

- **Model's creative brief should specify the rhetorical beat, not just layout type.** For every slide require `takeaway`, `evidence`, `visual_verb` (`compare`, `sequence`, `scale`, `locate`, `reveal`, `humanize`, `pause`, etc.), `primary_focal_object`, short copy, source and reason the visual treatment suits the beat. Permit large single-object, editorial photographic, quote, chart, spatial diagram, montage, and split-field compositions. A repeated card grid is one option, not the fallback.
- **Style extraction needs two passes:** native object/XML geometry with recursively traversed groups/media fills, then visual read-back of representative/all slides. Distinguish theme palette from local colors; capture observed modes (cover, dense, pause, close), alignment anchors, gutter ranges, logo/navigation layers, type *relative scale* and crop behavior. Save the native source's slide indexes and reference URLs; don't fabricate precision for preview-only material.
- **A grid is a constraint with exceptions.** Extract strong alignments (Sales Kickoff: edge ~0.4 in, six-cell gutters ~0.4 in; Corporate: content x≈2 in; SaaS: stable x=0.73 in header/footer; Grayscale: top rule and x=16.11 folio), then allow approved focal assets to breach frames deliberately. Do not auto-flag every overlap as a bug; judge z-order and content clarity.
- **Composition/rhythm gate:** inspect the deck as a sequence: number of consecutive equal-card pages, density alternation, change of focal scale, color mode/photography changes and emotional placement. At least one page should earn a distinct idea-specific visual grammar, not simply palette alternation.
- **Template mode has two meaningful variants:** literal native-template following (preserve brand/layout/rights and edit the source safely) versus style translation (rebuild editable geometry using extracted grammar). Do not imply one when actually doing the other. These sampled decks' all-BLANK layouts and slide-level grouped artwork make naive layout-placeholder filling ineffective.
- **Native-editable means verify per object.** Text, shapes and built charts can be native. The 3D/rendered art and some "charts" in these PPTX sources are bitmap/embedded image material. Record flattened elements and whether source art usage is allowed; never promise the whole page is editable because the container is a `.pptx`.
- **Deterministic gate before export:** line-wrap and fit at the actual font in the actual output viewer; keep number/unit together; check table/chart labels and source boxes; verify repeated anchor alignment; flag dense text smaller than the audience/context allows. Render full slides plus a sequence contact sheet; inspect exported PDF *and* PPTX. The Sales Kickoff and Corporate failures above are concrete tests to put in a regression suite.
- **No invented claims and no mechanical copying of placeholders.** Attribution slides, stock faces, invented firms and fake performance numbers are design samples, not factual inputs. Document which visual inspiration was used and which assets are cleared for the actual audience.

## Cross-template taste base: what 13 source PPTX files actually repeat

- **Canvas and composition:** all 13 native downloads are 20 x 11.25 in, 16:9. This is an *export dimension*, not an instruction to hard-code 20-inch slides into every future presentation. The most frequent substantive edge anchor is ~0.4-1.2 in across dense sales/learning decks; many editorial/photo designs use 1.9-3 in inset, and display or photo objects intentionally bleed beyond the canvas. Asymmetrical split fields, 2-, 3- and 4-column grids and a small number of aligned recurring anchors beat arbitrary independent boxes. Specific measured examples: Sales Kickoff 3-column metric cards start x=0.41/6.93/13.47; Corporate proof cards x=1.99/7.60/13.21; SaaS agenda 3-column x=10.68/13.61/16.55; IT proposal margin x=0.42; Grayscale photo strip x=0.92. **The rule is consistent anchors and gutters *within a visual system*, not one universal margin.**
- **Type hierarchy:** source files frequently use display extremes: 64-120 pt on 20-inch canvases, occasional 144-300 pt hero statistics and even >600 pt decorative numerals; routine body often 18-32 pt, and tiny footer/navigation 12-21 pt. A useful ratio is hero roughly 3-5 x body and supporting copy ~0.6-0.8 x the focal number, not fixed absolute points. Translate to physical slide size/viewing context and viewer font availability. Some custom display faces (Michroma, Bungee, Ledger) are dependent on installed fonts; Inter/DM Sans/Nunito Sans supply body stability in their own source contexts. Multiple sampled decks already render clipped when font metrics change.
- **Color roles:** strong decks distribute color as *field and rhythm*: stable neutral ink/background, a dominant brand/character color, a rarer contrast/accent reserved for a section or key assertion, sometimes a second high-chroma visual layer. Sales Kickoff is white/pale with alternating neon 3D imagery; SaaS rotates full-bleed saturated fields but anchors content with charcoal and fixed footer; Grayscale removes hue and adds variety through photos/space; Media Plan switches dark section fields against cream content; e-commerce inverts purple/lime for section breaks. Avoid random equal-weight accents on every object, and do not confuse theme XML with explicit slide colors.
- **Images:** 13/13 source decks include media assets, even where top-level picture count is tiny. Photos, geometric raster art, colored glows, collages and grouped image fills are distinct jobs: humanize a claim, establish place/product, create sectional pause, or energize a measurable fact. This is not permission to select generic stock: image subject, crop, provenance and editability still matter. Collage appears repeatedly (Gray, Media Plan, Clean Minimal, Product Launch) but alternates with solo hero visuals; 3D illustration often crosses panel borders deliberately.
- **Structural rhythm:** most source decks include an agenda, then at least one clear section divider or reset, one topic-specific body format, and a closing/credits sequence. Not all have a four-card grid. The content-sensitive formats include statement hero, visual process/timeline, two-state comparison, table, large-number proof, gallery/photo story, quote pause, audience activity, CTA. Human designers repeat a motif (rule, badge, rail, footer, crop logic), **not the same page structure each time**.
- **Native representation gap:** 13/13 content decks select BLANK layout; all still carry masters/theme parts. Most visual complexity is per-slide shapes/groups and image assets rather than semantic chart/SmartArt packages. Several have basic tables, but visually impressive charts are often images/shapes. This is a limitation of this *template sample*, not of PowerPoint. A renderer should use native chart/table/diagram features when the task requires editable data, rather than imitate the sample's flattened charts.
- **Quality signals are conditional, not automatic:** professionally presented source decks can contain off-canvas decorative text and visibly broken wording/number units under LibreOffice. Learn their successful pace and composition while refusing their layout bugs; reference quality does not obviate final viewer-specific visual QA.


## PowerPoint/PresentationML is larger than this template sample

Official Microsoft Open XML documentation describes a `.pptx` as an OPC ZIP package: the presentation part (`ppt/presentation.xml`) orders slides and refers to masters; each slide has a part and relationships to layout, notes, images, charts, audio/video and other resources. Slide layouts derive from masters, and theme parts define defaults that can be overridden by local slide/run formatting. This section maps that *available feature surface* against the 13 inspected source files. It is **not** a claim that every feature is implemented by ProjectArrakis, nor that a missing feature in these samples is unsupported by PowerPoint. [O1-O8]

| Feature and native representation | What 13 PPTX templates actually contain | Engine opportunity / compatibility caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **Masters, layout inheritance, placeholders**: slide -> layout -> master relationship chain; master supplies persistent visuals/text defaults, named layouts and placeholder geometry; slide placeholders can inherit styling. [O1, O2] | Every inspected file has one master and 4-19 layout parts, but **all 228 content slides** use a layout named BLANK. Slide XML contains no `<p:ph>` placeholder elements. Header/footer motifs are usually copied or grouped at slide level. | A better new deck can generate meaningful title/section/body layouts and use placeholders for true brand reuse; when adapting these exact PPTX sources, editing a master alone won't update copied per-slide objects. Preserve relationship IDs and inheritance while modifying native source. |
| **Themes**: theme parts hold color schemes, major/minor fonts and effects; `schemeClr` can refer to theme roles, while explicit `srgbClr` overrides them. [O1] | All 13 have theme parts, usually two `ppt/theme/theme*.xml` files. Several use visible direct RGB instead of their generic theme's accent scheme; Sales Kickoff's theme `accent1=058DC7` does not describe the neon palette visible in slides. | Store both theme tokens and observed local palette. Theme-aware color generation is valuable for long-term rebranding, but a source audit must resolve effective RGB and relative luminance at shape/run level. |
| **Native shapes, freeforms, lines, groups, images and crops**: drawing-tree objects with transforms, fills, effects and relationships. [O1] | Every file uses images; e.g. Product Launch has 64 image assets but only one top-level picture, because additional visuals occur as filled shapes/groups. Clean Minimal has 118 top-level line shapes; source designs frequently layer group/freeform and picture. | Use native text/shape/diagram objects where possible, image layers only for art/photos; recursively inspect group transforms, media relationships, crop/fill and z-order before claiming a full-image or editable layout. |
| **Tables**: `a:tbl` is editable grid, not a flattened picture. [O1] | Four native tables: Sales Soft Skills slide 4 (6 rows x 2 columns, hard/soft skills); Corporate slide 15 (resource page 4 x 2); SaaS Proposal and IT Proposal each have a resource/credits table. Most visually similar metric grids are separate text/shape groups. | Use native tables for dense comparable categorical values or data a user may revise. Keep column widths, cell insets, row height, typography and contrast deliberate; tables should not replace hero infographic composition automatically. |
| **Charts**: `ppt/charts/chart*.xml` with graphic-frame reference, optional embedded workbook/data cache, chart-specific layout/style. [O1] | **Zero chart parts and zero chart references** across all 13. Corporate slide 10's bar chart is a top-level picture. Software Company slide 11 has grouped visual bars/bitmaps, not a native chart. | For actual quantitative deliverables, create native editable charts with verified data and labels. Source artwork isn't proof of editability. Preserve data workbook/chart relationships, validate series/axes and render; a PDF picture is not an equivalent native chart. |
| **SmartArt / diagrams**: diagram data, drawing, layout/color/style parts plus `a:graphicData` and relationships. [O1] | **Zero `ppt/diagrams` parts.** The proposal/process/timeline illustrations are groups, text, lines and photos; four `a:graphicData` cases correspond to native tables, not SmartArt. | SmartArt is available in the format for flexible organizational/process visuals, but may reflow between Office/LibreOffice. Consider when editability and supported viewers justify it; otherwise native independent shapes/connectors may be more predictable. |
| **Slide transitions**: `p:transition` in slide XML; duration, advance and effect child. [O4] | 9/13 files carry them. Corporate 14 push; SaaS 12 push; Sales Closing 13 push; Minimalist E-Commerce 18 fade; Product Launch 16 fade; Graphic Design 18 fade; Clean Minimal 24 push; Software Company 18 push; Media Plan 17 push. Sales Kickoff, Grayscale and IT Proposal have none. (Counts exclude final or other slides lacking the element.) | Motion can reinforce chapter pacing, but is optional and may vanish in PDF. If implemented, specify restrained timing/direction and test in PowerPoint playback, not only PDF raster output. |
| **Object animation/timing**: slide `p:timing` animation tree can target shapes; sequence, click/with/after triggers and motion/effect settings. [O3] | **Zero slide `p:timing` elements** across 13 native files. The 3D assets and blurred effects are static designs despite their implied motion. | A future engine can reveal a process or build a comparison in stages for live talks, but should offer static PDF fallback and test motion separately; do not infer native animation from a dynamic-looking still. |
| **Speaker notes and notes master**: each slide can relate to a `notesSlide` with presenter-only text/objects; a notes master supplies defaults. [O5] | Each of the 228 slides has a notes-slide part and each deck one notes master, but the notes parts contain **no substantive speaker-note text** in this sample. This is structural export boilerplate, not evidence of prepared narration. | Place source traceability, delivery cues and accessibility explanations in real notes when useful. Distinguish empty notes parts from authored notes; do not leak private notes in a deck shared widely. |
| **Sections**: `p14:sectionLst` extension in presentation properties groups slides for navigation. [O6] | No section elements in the inspected presentation XML/properties; apparent chapter breaks are only visual slides. | Add true sections for a long editable deck where presenter navigation matters, but do not assume a styled divider automatically means an Office section. Some readers may ignore extension parts. |
| **Audio, video, embedding, OLE and hyperlinks**: media/embedding parts with per-slide relationships; text/shape hyperlinks can target slide or external URL. [O1, O7, O8] | No embedded audio/video references, no `ppt/embeddings` parts and no actual embedded media beyond images. Hyperlink elements do exist: SaaS Proposal has ten internal slide links; IT Proposal seven; Sales Closing eight external links. Many 'Back to agenda' labels have no actual hyperlink relationship in the sampled decks. | Use video/audio only when delivery context and offline playback allow; ensure poster image and PDF alternative. For interactive agenda/navigation, create and test real hyperlink targets, not label-only affordances. Sanitize external links and document privacy implications. |
| **Comments, metadata, handouts**: comment/author parts and handout-master formats exist; document metadata describes author/edit state. [O1] | No slide comment or handout master parts in this 13-file set; credits/resource slides are ordinary content, not handout or comment parts. | Native comments can support review workflows but should be stripped/resolved for final distribution when inappropriate. Handout layouts differ from projector slides and may need independent QA. Preserve useful author/core metadata without accidentally exposing private drafts. |

**Count definition:** source ZIP part names and slide XML were scanned for `p:transition`, `p:timing`, `p:ph`, `a:tbl`, `a:graphicData`, hyperlinks and media relationships. 228 = 16+15+14+22+14+14+17+19+19+25+16+19+18. A named format feature may appear in alternate namespaces or extensions; this inventory is specific to these ZIPs and the documented checks, not a universal validator. No actual PPTX was available for the Slidesgo preview or four Canva-only shortlist pages, so they are excluded from feature counts.

**Feature priorities for an engine:** (1) reliable native text/shapes/images and effective theme palette plus deterministic fit; (2) reusable actual masters/layouts/placeholders, native chart/table and validated internal links where content demands them; (3) notes/sections/animations/embedded media only when the user's delivery setting benefits and player-specific QA is available. Exposing every feature is not itself better design: assign it to a message and audience need.
