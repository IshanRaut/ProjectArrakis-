## Native-deck finding: layout as a persistent broadcast overlay

Source: SlidesCarnival, [Interactive News](https://www.slidescarnival.com/template/interactive-news-slides/239820), [official native PPTX](https://www.slidescarnival.com/download/sc02791/interactive-news-slides/pptx). I downloaded its 15-slide, 149 MB PPTX (SHA-256 `3535f910899e40bda5fa9e11274ef4f7ded86d5e2667460c4f65936a3ec1cc60`), parsed native objects and slide XML with python-pptx/lxml, and visually reviewed the official site's slide images for pages 2-13. **The native deck did not finish LibreOffice export within the bounded attempt**, so those site images are visual evidence only; the OOXML claims below come from the actual downloaded PPTX. This is not an award-winning provenance claim. It is a distinctly non-generic layout case for the engine's design vocabulary.

**New vs the existing baseline:** the 13-deck knowledge base covers recurring footers, agenda rails, dark/light section changes and photo collages. This news deck treats the presentation as a *broadcast frame* whose persistent on-screen layers establish source, state and urgency while the story content changes behind them. A black ticker strip starts at y=10.54 in on a 20 x 11.25 in slide and spans the full width on slides 2-13. Five native text labels ('STOCKS 100%', clearly demo content and not real data) repeat at x=0.40, 4.71, 9.18, 13.64 and 17.40 on that strip, interleaved with small trend marks. Separate lower thirds carry 'BREAKING NEWS' (slide 5 x=0.87, y=8.96; slide 8 x=0.71, y=8.28), 'JUST IN' (slide 7 x=13.29, y=8.09), a clock-like '20:00' (slide 6 x=15.55, y=8.91) or a segment title (slide 10 x=7.33, y=9.96). A badge 'YOUR/NEWS' moves around the frame rather than occupying one fixed corner. The main field varies among single portrait, live split-screen, four-panel event grid, quote over media, and a data/proof page (slides 3-12 on the official previews). The repeated overlay is a *functional information layer*, not ornament.

**Engine contract:** when a brief calls for a live update, sports/event recap or 'bulletin' feel, an agentic planner may choose this *broadcast* style and assign roles to (1) main evidence/video still, (2) lower-third claim/state, (3) source/network badge, (4) ticker with verified short data, and (5) an optional live-time/status label. Treat each as an editable slot with a fact source and expiration. For a timeless client pitch or when no real ticker data exists, omit the ticker instead of copying fake moving-stocks text. Prioritize the story field, then state, then attribution; keep these separate from decorative media so the planner can swap a photo without silently changing the claim. This is a content-role grammar, not a universal template or a command to add a news strip to every slide.

**Native caveat:** all 15 slides use the BLANK layout. The file has 158 top-level groups, 137 text boxes, 17 freeforms, 15 top-level pictures and 65 media parts. It has **zero** slide timing/animation elements, transition elements and hyperlinks in the inspected XML. Despite the 'Interactive' title and 'LIVE' badges, it is a static presentation in this export, not a live feed or clickable UI. A renderer should never promise interactivity or real-time market data from the labels alone. Preview screenshots and native text show several tiny ticker labels; use target-viewer QA and a legible static fallback. Source demo images may have third-party licenses: review current [SlidesCarnival terms](https://www.slidescarnival.com/terms-of-use) and retain required credits/replace media before reuse. Do not copy its news photos into generic engine assets.

## Video-derived judgment tests (not new hard rules)

1. [Nancy Duarte, TEDxEast, 'The secret structure of great talks'](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1nYFpuc2Umk): I inspected its English subtitle track. Duarte describes a talk's 'what is' vs 'what could be' contrast, recurring resistance, a final call to action and a 'new bliss' ending, using the 2007 iPhone launch and 'I Have a Dream' as examples. The engine already values story-first and varied slide roles; useful refinement is to ask the planner to **tag the narrative job** of each selected source slide before editing: establish present state, show possible state, handle resistance/proof, pivotal demonstration, ask/ending. A gallery or quote slide without a narrative job is not justified by its good looks. This is a persuasive-talk model from one expert, not a mandated arc for every informational deck.
2. [Analyst Academy, 'How to Build PowerPoint Slides Like a Graphic Designer'](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xxw5DYTVZSc): I inspected available English subtitles. Its examples explain attention order via relative size/weight, whitespace as emphasis and grouping, and alignment as an implied relationship between objects. Existing knowledge covers anchors and type hierarchy, so no duplicate 'use hierarchy' pattern is added. The operational test is more precise: after an edit, ask what gets noticed first, whether that is the intended assertion, whether whitespace preserves groups, and whether alignment accidentally binds unrelated items. These are judgment/visual-QA checks, not fixed margins or point sizes.
3. [From Bland to Brand: How I Redesign Slides With AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zmlPawRqcu4): subtitle-only review of a worked AI slide-redesign demo. It starts from a human-authored content sequence, requests per-slide diagnosis, then changes an uninformative heading to an insight-led assertion, removes surplus content, changes one layout, and adjusts a stock-photo focal crop. This supports *inspect -> plan -> edit -> re-inspect* rather than a single 'generate slides' call. Its suggested numeric rules (word count, bullets, font sizes, color count) are presenter heuristics, not universal limits; do not hard-code them across 20-inch templates or different viewing contexts. I did not validate the final deck's rendered fidelity or the demo's research claims.
