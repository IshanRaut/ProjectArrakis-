## Learning cycle 2: comic-panel storytelling as a layout grammar (26 September 2026)

**New native source [SC14]:** [SlidesCarnival, The Human Spider Comic Style](https://www.slidescarnival.com/template/spider-comic-background/53846), [official PPTX download](https://www.slidescarnival.com/download/sc00790/the-human-spider-comic-style-slides/pptx). Downloaded the 62.2 MB source PPTX (SHA-256 `962273cd6d430c963c276d8523c24ca7335fca874e11be3049eb8cb9be6a7b2e`), inspected native shapes/XML on a 20 x 11.25 in canvas, rendered all 23 slides in LibreOffice and visually inspected slides 1-21 as a montage. Twenty-one slides are content/template pages; the last two are resources and credits. This is a **style-specific option**, not a default skin for corporate presentations. Source characters, example numbers and imagery are not facts or licensed reusable engine assets.

**Novel pattern: the slide is a comic page, not a canvas of generic cards.** Existing knowledge covers photo collages, editorial grids, section dividers and images crossing panel borders. This source adds a *semantic panel grammar*: a black-outlined white caption box for explanation (slides 5, 9, 10, 16-17); explosive white/red/yellow speech or starburst fields for revelation, chapter titles or the final ask (4, 8, 14, 19-21); hard black gutters and differently sized illustrated frames for simultaneous scenes (3, 11-12, 18); halftone/web/radial backgrounds for dramatic energy. Slide 3 maps an agenda into three equal vertical story panels. Slide 6 breaks that grid into one left proposition and three right stacked episode strips, each with an icon and line of text. Slide 11 is an asymmetric four-image page: one dominant image on the left, two small top-right panels and one wide bottom-right panel. The repeated *inked border* and saturated red/blue/yellow/orange unite these different geometries. A comic speech bubble is a role for a line of dialogue or dramatic claim, **not** just another rounded rectangle.

**Native mechanism and testable contract:** the file has 81 top-level pictures, 46 text boxes, 37 freeforms and 29 groups. Most full-bleed pages use large picture layers and many character illustrations are pictures, while captions/headings are separate editable text or grouped objects. For example, slide 11's hero image begins x=0.24, y=2.48, w=9.73, h=8.49; its right-side images begin x=10.30 and 15.31, with the lower strip x=10.33, y=8.37, w=9.42. Slide 6's three recurring right-hand groups start at x=8.97, y=0.02/3.84/7.43; the left proposition is x=1.06, y=4.23. Preserve gutters, thick outlines and overlap hierarchy when scaling. An engine should select a *scene/page archetype* (three-panel agenda, burst divider, asymmetric scene collage, caption-plus-illustration, full-page concluding burst), then allocate narrative roles to panels. It should not tile each talking point into identical cards. Keep headings/captions native and selectable where possible; render the finished page to check contrast and text fit against busy art. The file puts content on BLANK layouts again, so its visual grammar is a slide-level arrangement, not a master/layout placeholder scheme.

**Visual QA warning:** the LibreOffice render shows some source text too small for a projected talk (slides 8-10, 15-16) and the slide 4 word wraps awkwardly inside its burst. Learn the scene rhythm, not the broken copy fit. Source images include recognizable comic-character art: **do not ship the extracted images as reusable engine assets** without separate rights review. For a real deck, use commissioned/licensed imagery and keep source credit terms distinct from native pattern analysis.
